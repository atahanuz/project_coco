Inside the swarm

About these numbers
  • Problems: the 207 IMO-AnswerBench problems outside geometry whose answers can be checked automatically (54 algebra, 91 combinatorics, 62 number theory).
  • One answer: the model thinks once, up to 64k tokens, and gives a final answer. E4B has 8 answers per problem; 12B has 8 for 168 problems and 6 or 7 for the rest.
  • Majority vote: the most common of a model's answers to a problem; a tie counts as half right.
  • Solo agent: one model in an agent loop with a Python tool. Swarm: a master assigns tasks to W workers for up to three rounds; workers can run Python, write to shared notes, and report a candidate answer; the master decides the final answer.
  • Ablations: one round only (no follow-up rounds), and no shared notes (workers see only their own task).
  • The 12B solo agent covers 154 of the 207 problems; every other approach covers all 207. The E4B solo agent, 4-worker swarm and the two ablations ran twice per problem; the 2- and 8-worker swarms and the 12B agents ran once.
  • The 12B swarm ran with a per-call limit of 16k tokens: 58% of its worker calls hit that limit, and 15 problems ended with no answer. A rerun with double the limit on the 64 hardest problems removes all 9 missing answers there but does not raise accuracy (44% → 42% solved; 7 of the 9 become wrong answers), so the limit is not what holds it back.
  • Answers are checked as exact numbers or sets of numbers. A closed form such as 3^{25}+1 counts as wrong even when it is equal to the correct number; this affects under 1% of answers and no approach by more than 0.7 points.
