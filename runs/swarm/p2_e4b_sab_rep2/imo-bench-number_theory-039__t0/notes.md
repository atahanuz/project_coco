$$(1+b)c^2 + (b^2 - 9b + 2)c + (1+b)^2 = 0$$
For $c$ to be an integer, the discriminant $\Delta$ must be a perfect square $D^2$.
$$\Delta = (b^2 - 9b + 2)^2 - 4(1+b)^3$$
We require $D^2 = (b^2 - 9b + 2)^2 - 4(1+b)^3$.
Let $f(b) = (b^2 - 9b + 2)^2 - 4(1+b)^3$. This is a polynomial of degree 4 in $b$.
The requirement is to find integral points $(b, D)$ on the hyperelliptic curve $D^2 = f(b)$. By Siegel's theorem on integral points, since $f(b)$ is a polynomial of degree $\ge 3$, this curve has only a finite number of integer solutions $(b, D)$.
Therefore, there are only finitely many pairs $(b, c)$ satisfying the condition when $a=1$ and $n=3$. This strongly confirms the claim that $n=3$ does not yield infinitely many solutions in the $a=1$ case.
