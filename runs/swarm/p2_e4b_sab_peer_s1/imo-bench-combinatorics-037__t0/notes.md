Goal: Prove $R \subseteq S$.

Proof:
Let $n$ be an arbitrary element of $R$.
By definition, $n$ must be of the form $n = 11a + 20b$, where $a$ and $b$ are non-negative integers ($\mathbb{N}_0$).

We proceed by induction on $a+b$.

Base Case: $a+b = 0$.
If $a=0$ and $b=0$, then $n = 0$. By the definition of a Bachata subset, $0 \in S$. The base case holds.

Inductive Step: Assume that for all $n' \in R$ such that the sum of the coefficients generating $n'$ is less than $k$, we have $n' \in S$.
Consider $n \in R$ such that $n = 11a + 20b$ and $a+b = k \ge 1$.

Case 1: $a \ge 1$.
Let $n' = n - 11 = 11(a-1) + 20b$. Since $a-1 \ge 0$ and $b \ge 0$, $n' \in R$.
Since $a-1 + b = k-1 < k$, by the induction hypothesis, $n' \in S$.
Since $n' \in S$ and $n = n' + 11$, by the second condition of the Bachata set definition, $n \in S$.

Case 2: $a = 0$ (and thus $b \ge 1$).
Let $n' = n - 20 = 11(0) + 20(b-1)$. Since $b-1 \ge 0$, $n' \in R$.
Since $0 + (b-1) = k-1 < k$, by the induction hypothesis, $n' \in S$.
Since $n' \in S$ and $n = n' + 20$, by the second condition of the Bachata set definition, $n \in S$.

Conclusion: By mathematical induction, any element $n \in R$ must be in $S$. Therefore, $R \subseteq S$.
The proof is complete.
