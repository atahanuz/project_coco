1. Structural Constraint in $\mathbb{Z}_n[x]$:
The condition $P(k) \equiv 0 \pmod n$ for all $k \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ implies that $P(x)$ evaluates to $0$ for every element in the ring $\mathbb{Z}_n$. That is, $P(x)$ is the zero function when restricted to $\mathbb{Z}_n$.

2. Implications for Polynomial Form:
If $n$ were a prime number $p$, then $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is a field. Since $\deg(P) = p > 1$, the only way a polynomial of degree $p$ can vanish on all $p$ elements of $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is if it is divisible by the polynomial $x^p - x$ in $\mathbb{Z}_p[x]$.
Thus, for prime $n=p$:
$P(x) \equiv c(x^p - x) \pmod p$, where $c$ is the leading coefficient of $P(x)$ (which must be $1$ or $-1$ since $a_p \neq 0$).

For composite $n$, the situation is more complex because $\mathbb{Z}_n$ is not a field. The ideal of polynomials that vanish on all of $\mathbb{Z}_n$ is generated by specific functions related to the Chinese Remainder Theorem decomposition of $\mathbb{Z}_n$. However, the fundamental constraint remains that $P(x)$ must be congruent modulo $n$ to a polynomial that vanishes on all of $\mathbb{Z}_n$. If $n$ is square-free, $n=p_1 p_2 \cdots p_r$, then $P(x) \equiv 0 \pmod{p_i}$ for all $i$. This forces $P(x)$ to be structurally related to $x^{p_i} - x$ modulo $p_i$.
