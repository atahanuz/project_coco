**Analysis of George's Process (G)**

George's sequence $A_G = (a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_m)$ is determined by a greedy algorithm aimed at minimizing the magnitude of consecutive partial sums.

1.  **Initialization ($i=1$):** $a_1$ is chosen such that $|a_1| = \min_{a \in A} |a|$. Let $A_{rem}$ be the set of remaining numbers.
2.  **Greedy Step ($i>1$):** Let $S_{i-1} = \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} a_j$. George chooses $a_i \in A_{rem}$ such that $|S_{i-1} + a_i|$ is minimized.
    Mathematically, $a_i$ is chosen to minimize $|S_{i-1} + a_i| = \min_{a \in A_{rem}} |S_{i-1} + a|$. This is equivalent to choosing $a_i$ closest to $-S_{i-1}$.

**Structure:** The sequence $A_G$ attempts to maintain the property that all partial sums $S_k$ are close to zero. Specifically, $G = \max_k |S_k|$.

**Bounding $G$:** We seek bounds on $G$. Since $S_m = \sum_{i=1}^m a_i$ (the total sum $\Sigma$) is fixed regardless of arrangement, we must have $G \ge |\Sigma|$.

The primary question is: How close can $G$ get to $|\Sigma|$?
If all numbers are zero except one, $G$ is large. If the numbers are balanced, $G$ should be small.

We need to test small cases to understand the relationship between $G$ and the input set.
