An assignment $A$ can be viewed as a permutation $\sigma \in S_N$ applied to the fixed vertex positions (labeled $v_1, v_2, \dots, v_N$ in cyclic order). Since the problem states that assignments coinciding under rotation are treated as the same, we must consider the action of the cyclic group $C_N$ on the set of all permutations $S_N$.

Let $R$ be the rotation operator: $R(\sigma) = \sigma \circ r$, where $r$ is the cyclic shift permutation $(1\ 2\ \dots\ N)$.
The set of all possible assignments, $A$, is the set of orbits of $S_N$ under the action of $C_N$:
$$A = S_N / C_N$$
The size of the set of assignments is $|A| = N! / N = (N-1)!$.

## 2. The Distance Function ($d$)

The allowed operation is swapping the integers assigned to a pair of adjacent vertices. This corresponds to applying an adjacent transposition in $S_N$.

Let $A_1$ and $A_2$ be two assignments (two orbits in $A$). We seek the minimum number of adjacent swaps required to transform an assignment in $A_1$ to an assignment in $A_2$.

Let $\sigma_1 \in A_1$ and $\sigma_2 \in A_2$. The distance $d(\sigma_1, \sigma_2)$ between two specific linear assignments is the minimum number of adjacent transpositions needed to transform $\sigma_1$ into $\sigma_2$.

$$d(A_1, A_2) = \min_{\sigma'_1 \in A_1, \sigma'_2 \in A_2} d_{lin}(\sigma'_1, \sigma'_2)$$
where $d_{lin}$ is the minimum number of adjacent swaps required, considering the cyclical structure.

Due to the cyclic arrangement, the distance $d_{lin}$ is not simply the inversion count based on a linear ordering, but rather the minimum distance over all $N$ possible "cuts" of the cycle.

The problem asks for the diameter of the graph defined by $A$:
$$n = \max_{A_1, A_2 \in A} d(A_1, A_2)$$

## 3. Conjectural Connection

The problem is equivalent to finding the diameter of the Cayley graph of $S_N$ restricted by the rotational symmetry $C_N$, where generators are the adjacent transpositions (on $C_N$).
