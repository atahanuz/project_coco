### [W3, round 1]
Analysis of Good Triples $(x, y, z)$ where $|x|, |y|, |z| \ge 2$:

The condition is $y^x z^y x^z = p$, where $p$ is a positive prime number.

1.  **Requirement for Integer Result (p):** For $y^x, z^y, x^z$ to be integers (since $p$ is an integer prime), we must have non-negative exponents if the bases are not $\pm 1$. Since we require $|x|, |y|, |z| \ge 2$, this forces $x \ge 2$, $y \ge 2$, and $z \ge 2$. If any exponent were negative (e.g., $x<0$), the corresponding term would be a fraction (e.g., $y^x = 1/y^{|x|}$), meaning the product $p$ could not be an integer prime.

2.  **Analysis for Positive Integers ($x, y, z \ge 2$):**
    If $x, y, z \ge 2$, then the factors $y^x, z^y, x^z$ are all integers greater than 1.
    Since $|x|, |y|, |z| \ge 2$:
    *   $y^x \ge 2^2 = 4$
    *   $z^y \ge 2^2 = 4$
    *   $x^z \ge 2^2 = 4$
    The product $p = y^x z^y x^z$ is a product of three integers, all strictly greater than 1. Therefore, $p$ must be a composite number, contradicting the definition that $p$ is a prime number.

**Conclusion:** There are no good triples $(x, y, z)$ satisfying the constraint that $|x|, |y|, |z| \ge 2$. The set of such triples is empty.

### [W3, Focus Task] Rigorous analysis of good triples where components are $\pm 2$

**Task:** Prove or disprove that no good triples exist where $|x|=|y|=|z|=2$.
**Constraint:** $P = y^x z^y x^z$ must be a positive prime number.
**Assumption:** $x, y, z \in \{-2, 2\}$.

1.  **Sign analysis:** Since $x, y, z$ are even (either $\pm 2$), if a base is negative (e.g., $y=-2$) and the exponent is even (e.g., $x=2$), the term $y^x$ is positive: $(-2)^2 = 4$. Therefore, the product $P = y^x z^y x^z$ must be positive regardless of the signs of $x, y, z$.
2.  **Magnitude analysis:** Since the bases are $\pm 2$, we can rewrite $P$ using absolute values:
    $P = |y|^x |z|^y |x|^z = 2^x \cdot 2^y \cdot 2^z = 2^{x+y+z}$.
3.  **Primality requirement:** For $P$ to be an integer prime number, $P$ must equal $2^1$. This requires the exponent $S = x+y+z$ to equal 1.
4.  **Checking possible sums:** Since $x, y, z \in \{-2, 2\}$, the possible sums $S$ are:
    *   $S = 2+2+2 = 6$. $P = 2^6 = 64$. (Composite)
    *   $S = 2+2-2 = 2$. $P = 2^2 = 4$. (Composite)
    *   $S = 2-2-2 = -2$. $P = 2^{-2} = 1/4$. (Not an integer)
    *   $S = -2-2-2 = -6$. $P = 2^{-6} = 1/64$. (Not an integer)
5.  **Conclusion:** The required sum $x+y+z=1$ is impossible when $x, y, z \in \{-2, 2\}$. Therefore, no good triples exist where all components are $\pm 2$. This confirms the general conclusion derived earlier that no good triples exist where $|x|, |y|, |z| \ge 2$.
