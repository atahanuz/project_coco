### [W4, round 1]
**Combinatorial Approach for Weak Quartets (WQs)**

A weak quartet is a set of 4 vertices $\{u, v, x, y\}$ containing exactly one edge. We count these by summing over every possible edge $e = \{u, v\}$.

1. **Edge Selection:** Choose an edge $e=\{u, v\} \in E$.
2. **Vertex Selection:** We must choose two additional vertices $\{x, y\}$ from the set $R_{uv}$, where $R_{uv}$ contains all vertices not adjacent to $u$ or $v$ (and not $u$ or $v$ themselves).
   $R_{uv} = V \setminus (N[u] \cup N[v])$, where $N[z]$ is the closed neighborhood of $z$.
3. **Counting Pairs:** For $\{u, v, x, y\}$ to be a weak quartet with edge $\{u, v\}$, $\{x, y\}$ must not be an edge in the graph $G$.
   Let $r_{uv} = |R_{uv}|$. The number of ways to choose $\{x, y\}$ is the number of non-edges (independent pairs) in the subgraph induced by $R_{uv}$, denoted $G[R_{uv}]$.

**Expression Formulation:**
Let $c_{uv} = |N(u) \cap N(v)|$ be the number of common neighbors of $u$ and $v$.
The size of the remote set $R_{uv}$ is:
$r_{uv} = N - d_u - d_v + c_{uv}$, where $N=120$.

Let $E_{uv} = |E(G[R_{uv}])|$ be the number of edges within $R_{uv}$.
The number of WQs generated by edge $\{u, v\}$ is $W_{uv} = \binom{r_{uv}}{2} - E_{uv}$.

The total number of weak quartets $W$ is:
$W = \sum_{\{u, v\} \in E} \left( \binom{N - d_u - d_v + c_{uv}}{2} - E_{uv} \right)$.

Note: $E_{uv}$ depends on the specific structure of the graph and cannot generally be expressed solely in terms of vertex degrees ($d_i$) or total edge count $|E|$.

### [W2, round 1]
Weak Quartet Analysis:
A weak quartet is a set of 4 vertices inducing exactly 1 edge.
Let N be the number of users (N=120) and M be the number of edges.

Derivation of the Upper Bound:
For any specific edge $e=\{u, v\} \in E$, we need to count how many pairs of vertices $\{x, y\}$ (from the remaining $N-2$ vertices) can be chosen such that $\{u, v, x, y\}$ forms a weak quartet based on $e$. This requires that $\{x, y\}$ is not an edge, and $x$ and $y$ are not connected to $u$ or $v$.
To maximize the count $W$ for a fixed $M$, we must assume the best possible local structure: that the edges are independent (form a matching), and critically, that there are no other edges whatsoever in the graph ($M$ is the total number of edges).

If the graph consists solely of $M$ disjoint edges, the number of weak quartets is M multiplied by the number of ways to choose 2 additional vertices from the remaining N-2 vertices, provided these two vertices are not connected. Since we assume no other edges exist, this condition is met.

Upper Bound Relationship:
The maximum possible number of weak quartets $W_{max}(M)$ for a given M is bounded by:
W <= M * C(N-2, 2)

For N=120, C(118, 2) = 6903.
W <= M * 6903.

Structural Intuition:
The number of weak quartets is maximized when the graph is sparse, specifically when the edges are spread out as much as possible (forming a matching). A dense graph is intuitively expected to minimize this count because the high density of edges makes it statistically unlikely to find 4 vertices with *exactly* one edge.

### [W1, round 1]
Analysis of Weak Quartets:
A weak quartet is an induced subgraph on 4 vertices containing exactly 1 edge. We analyze the maximum possible number for small N.

N=4: Total quartets = 1. Max weak quartets = 1, achieved when the graph has exactly 1 edge.
N=5: Total quartets = 5. Max weak quartets = 4, achieved by a graph with two disjoint edges, e.g., E = {(1, 2), (3, 4)}.
N=6: Total quartets = 15. Max weak quartets = 12, achieved by a graph with three disjoint edges, e.g., E = {(1, 2), (3, 4), (5, 6)}.

Observation: Maximizing the number of weak quartets appears to be achieved by structuring the graph using the maximum possible number of disjoint edges. Introducing additional edges tends to increase the edge count of induced quartets beyond 1, thus reducing the total count of weak quartets.

### [W3, round 1]
The number of weak quartets (a group of 4 vertices inducing exactly one edge) is calculated for two structured graph types on N=120 users.

1. Complete Bipartite Graph $K_{a, b}$ ($a+b=120$):
A quartet of vertices $S$ split into $k$ vertices from partition $A$ and $4-k$ from partition $B$ induces $k(4-k)$ edges. For $k \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}$, $k(4-k)$ is 0, 3, 4, 3, or 0, respectively. Since $k(4-k)$ is never 1, the number of weak quartets in any $K_{a, b}$ is 0.

2. Disjoint Union of Cliques $G = K_{n_1} \cup K_{n_2} \cup \dots \cup K_{n_m}$ ($\sum n_i = 120$):
The number of weak quartets $W$ is given by:
$W = \sum_{i=1}^m \binom{n_i}{2} \left( \sum_{j<k, j, k \ne i} n_j n_k \right)$.
We tested the case $m=4$ with equal sizes: $n_1=n_2=n_3=n_4=30$.
$W = 4 \times \binom{30}{2} \times (3 \times 30^2) = 4 \times 435 \times 2700 = 4,698,000$.
We also checked an unequal case $(40, 30, 30, 20)$ and found $W = 4,527,000$, which is smaller. The structure $K_{30} \cup K_{30} \cup K_{30} \cup K_{30}$ appears to maximize $W$ among small $m$.
