The condition for a set $X$ to be a good set is that for any $a, b \in X$ and any $i \in \mathbb{Z}$, the element $Q(i) = a^2 + iab + b^2$ must belong to $X$.

1.  **Case $i=0$:** $a^2 + b^2 \in X$. $X$ must be closed under the sum of squares of its elements.
2.  **Case $i=2$:** $a^2 + 2ab + b^2 = (a+b)^2 \in X$. If $a, b \in X$, then $(a+b)^2 \in X$.
3.  **Case $i=-2$:** $a^2 - 2ab + b^2 = (a-b)^2 \in X$. If $a, b \in X$, then $(a-b)^2 \in X$.

These findings imply that if $a, b \in X$, then not only are their sums and differences squared in $X$, but also the sum of their squares is in $X$. This suggests that $X$ must have properties related to rings where squaring and summation of squares are well-behaved, potentially related to number fields where $a^2 + iab + b^2$ corresponds to a generalized norm.

Furthermore, the forms $a^2 \pm ab + b^2$ are strongly related to the norm form in the ring of Eisenstein integers $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$, where $\omega = e^{2\pi i/3}$. Specifically, $N(a+b\omega) = a^2 - ab + b^2$ (up to sign/conjugate choice). The inclusion of all integer $i$ significantly generalizes this, allowing for $(a+b)^2$ and $(a-b)^2$ which are highly restrictive.
