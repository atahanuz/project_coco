**Claim:** The claim is true. $S$ must be equal to $G$.

**Proof Sketch:**
1.  **$G \subseteq S$:** The set $G$ is defined as the set of all numbers reachable from 0 by repeatedly adding 11 or 20. Since $S$ is a Bachata subset, $0 \in S$, and $S$ is closed under adding 11 and 20 (by definition), any element generated by this process (i.e., any element in $G$) must belong to $S$.
2.  **$S \subseteq G$:** Let $n \in S$. Since $S$ is a Bachata subset, $n$ must have been generated starting from $0$ by a finite sequence of additions of 11 and 20. If $n$ is reached after adding 11 exactly $k$ times and adding 20 exactly $m$ times (where $k, m \ge 0$), then $n = 0 + 11k + 20m$. This means $n \in G$.

Since $G \subseteq S$ and $S \subseteq G$, we conclude $S = G$.
The number of Bachata subsets of $\{0, 1, 2, \ldots\}$ is exactly 1.
