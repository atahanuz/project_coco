### [W2, round 1]
Structural analysis of $ab + ac + bc = n$:
1. The given equation is equivalent to $ab + ac + bc = n$, where $a=d_2, b=d_3, c=d_5$ are the second, third, and fifth smallest divisors of $n$.
2. Let $p$ be the smallest prime factor of $n$. Then $a = d_2 = p$.
3. We examined the case where the initial divisors are powers of $p$, namely $d_2=p, d_3=p^2$, which requires $n$ to be divisible by $p^2$ and $p^2$ must be smaller than the smallest prime factor $q > p$ of $n$.
4. Under the assumption that $d_2=p, d_3=p^2$ and $d_5=p^3$, the equation simplifies to $p(p^2) + p(p^3) + p^2(p^3) = n$, which gives $n = p^3 + p^4 + p^5 = p^3(1+p+p^2)$.
5. We found solutions when $M = 1+p+p^2$ is a prime number $q$. In this case, $n = p^3 q$.
6. Checking the divisor ordering for $n=p^3 q$, we found that if $p \ge 2$, the ordering of divisors is $d_1=1, d_2=p, d_3=p^2$, and $d_5=p^3$ (provided $p^3 > q$, which is true for $p \ge 2$). The fourth divisor $d_4$ is $q$.
7. This structure requires $n=p^3 q$ where $p$ and $q=p^2+p+1$ are prime, and $p < q$.
8. For all such solutions found ($n=56, 351$), the number of divisors is $\tau(n) = (3+1)(1+1) = 8$.
9. Further analysis of other divisor configurations (e.g., where $d_3$ or $d_5$ involve distinct prime factors) suggested highly restrictive constraints or failure to satisfy the equation (e.g., $n=72$). The strongest structural constraint points to $\tau(n)=8$.
