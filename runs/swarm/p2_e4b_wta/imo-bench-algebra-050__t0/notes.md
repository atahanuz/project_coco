### [W2, round 1]
**Method for Minimizing $\sum k_i$ (Minimizing the 'Weight' of Representation)**

1.  **Algebraic Reduction:** The root $r$ is algebraic of degree 3. This means the set $\{1, r, r^2\}$ forms a basis for the field extension $\mathbb{Q}(r)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.
    The defining relation is $r^3 = 10 - r^2 - r$. We can use this to reduce any power $r^i$ ($i \ge 3$) into a unique linear combination of $\{1, r, r^2\}$ with integer coefficients.
    We define the reduction function $R$: for any $i \ge 0$, there exist unique integers $a_{i,0}, a_{i,1}, a_{i,2}$ such that:
    $$r^i = a_{i,0} + a_{i,1} r + a_{i,2} r^2$$
    (Note: $R(r^0) = 1 \implies a_{0,0}=1, a_{0,1}=0, a_{0,2}=0$).

2.  **System Conversion:** We substitute this reduction into the given equation:
    $$\sum_{i=0}^{101} k_i r^i = 2025$$
    $$\sum_{i=0}^{101} k_i (a_{i,0} + a_{i,1} r + a_{i,2} r^2) = 2025 \cdot 1 + 0 \cdot r + 0 \cdot r^2$$

3.  **Diophantine Constraints:** Since $1, r, r^2$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}$, by equating the coefficients of the basis elements, we obtain a system of three linear Diophantine equations in the variables $k_0, k_1, \ldots, k_{101}$:
    $$\sum_{i=0}^{101} k_i a_{i,0} = 2025 \quad \text{(Coefficient of 1)}$$
    $$\sum_{i=0}^{101} k_i a_{i,1} = 0 \quad \text{(Coefficient of r)}$$
    $$\sum_{i=0}^{101} k_i a_{i,2} = 0 \quad \text{(Coefficient of } r^2 \text{)}$$

4.  **Optimization Goal:** The formal method requires finding a solution set $\{k_i\} \in (\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0})^{102}$ that satisfies the system of three constraints while minimizing the objective function:
    $$\text{Minimize } S = \sum_{i=0}^{101} k_i$$

**Algorithm Outline:**
The method translates the problem from an algebraic representation problem into an Integer Linear Programming (ILP) problem.
a. Calculate the coefficients $a_{i,j}$ for $i=0$ to $101$ using repeated application of the reduction rule ($r^{i+1} = r \cdot r^i$).
b. Solve the resulting constrained optimization problem, minimizing $\sum k_i$. Due to the large number of variables (102) and the structure of the equations, specialized ILP solvers would be needed to find the global minimum.

This formal method successfully translates the algebraic representation constraint into a solvable optimization framework.

### [W3, round 1]
**Necessary Conditions for $\sum_{i=0}^{101} k_i r^i = 2025$**

Let $r$ be the positive root of $x^3+x^2+x-10=0$. Since $\{1, r, r^2\}$ forms a $\mathbb{Q}$-basis for $\mathbb{Q}(r)$, we express $r^i = a_i + b_i r + c_i r^2$. The coefficients $(a_i, b_i, c_i)$ are generated by the recurrence derived from $r^3 = 10 - r^2 - r$.

The sum $S = \sum_{i=0}^{101} k_i r^i$ can be written as:
$$S = \left(\sum_{i=0}^{101} k_i a_i\right) + \left(\sum_{i=0}^{101} k_i b_i\right) r + \left(\sum_{i=0}^{101} k_i c_i\right) r^2$$

For $S$ to equal the integer 2025, the coefficients of $r$ and $r^2$ must vanish. The necessary conditions on the non-negative integer coefficients $k_i$ are:

1. **(Coefficient of $r$):** $\sum_{i=0}^{101} k_i b_i = 0$
2. **(Coefficient of $r^2$):** $\sum_{i=0}^{101} k_i c_i = 0$

These conditions, combined with $\sum_{i=0}^{101} k_i a_i = 2025$, restrict the feasible set for the problem. The sequences $\{b_i\}_{i=0}^{101}$ and $\{c_i\}_{i=0}^{101}$ were calculated using the recurrence relation.

**Objective:** Determine if $\deg(A) > 0$ can yield a larger $\sum a_j$ while satisfying $k_i \ge 0$.

**Method:** We established that $K(x) = \sum k_i x^i = A(x) P(x) + 2025$, where $P(x) = x^3+x^2+x-10$. The sum $S = \sum k_i = K(1) = 2025 - 7 A(1)$. Minimizing $S$ requires maximizing $A(1) = \sum a_j$.

**Case $\deg(A)=0$ (Linear Case, previously studied):**
$A(x) = a_0$. Constraints require $a_0 \le 202$. Maximum $A(1) = 202$. Minimum $S = 2025 - 7(202) = 611$.

**Case $\deg(A)=1$ (Quadratic Case):**
$A(x) = a_1 x + a_0$. We seek to maximize $a_1 + a_0$ subject to $k_i \ge 0$ (assuming integer $a_i$ for simplicity).
Constraints: $a_1 \ge 0$, $a_0 \le 202$, and $a_0 \ge 10a_1$.
To maximize $a_0+a_1$, we choose the maximum allowed $a_0=202$. This forces $202 \ge 10a_1$, so $a_1 \le 20.2$. We choose the maximum integer $a_1=20$.
Maximum $A(1) = 202 + 20 = 222$.
The resulting minimum sum is $S = 2025 - 7(222) = 2025 - 1554 = 471$.

**Conclusion:**
The investigation confirms the claim that minimizing $S$ requires maximizing $\sum a_j$. Furthermore, we have demonstrated that $\deg(A) > 0$ *can* yield a larger $\sum a_j$ (e.g., $222 > 202$) and therefore a smaller minimum value for $S$ (e.g., $471 < 611$) compared to the $\deg(A)=0$ case.
