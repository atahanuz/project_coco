**Task 1: Linear Arrangement Swaps (N=199)**
The minimum number of adjacent swaps required to transform one permutation to another in a linear arrangement is equal to the number of inversions between the two permutations. The maximum required number of swaps (the diameter of the graph of permutations) is achieved between the identity permutation and the fully reversed permutation, which is given by $\binom{N}{2} = \frac{N(N-1)}{2}$.
For $N=199$, the maximum number of linear swaps is $\frac{199 \times 198}{2} = 19701$.

**Task 2: Circular Constraint and Rotational Equivalence Analysis**
1.  **Circular Constraint:** The circular nature introduces a wrap-around adjacency, connecting vertex $N$ and vertex $1$. For the maximum distance of general permutations, this usually does not drastically change the maximum distance from the linear case, although specific local paths might be shorter.
2.  **Rotational Equivalence:** This is the dominant modification. We are looking for the diameter of the graph of orbits under rotation. If $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$ are two permutations, the required minimum swaps $n$ is $d(\text{Orbit}(\pi_1), \text{Orbit}(\pi_2)) = \min_{\pi_1' \in \text{Orbit}(\pi_1), \pi_2' \in \text{Orbit}(\pi_2)} d(\pi_1', \pi_2')$. By choosing the optimal representative from each orbit, we significantly reduce the maximal distance required compared to the linear case.

A precise determination of the diameter of this quotient graph for $N=199$ is highly complex. However, known results concerning transpositions on a circle suggest that the maximum distance, even when accounting for rotational freedom, remains $O(N^2)$, and is often bounded close to the linear case, unless $N$ is small or special constraints apply.

Given the structure of the problem (achieving *every* other assignment), $n$ must be the diameter. Since the linear maximum is 19701, $n \le 19701$. The rotational equivalence allows for a much smaller $n$. Further investigation into the structure of the quotient graph is needed to find the exact value.
