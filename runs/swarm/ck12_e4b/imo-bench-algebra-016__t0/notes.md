The optimal sequence $A^*=(a^*_1, \ldots, a^*_m)$ aims to minimize the global maximum partial sum. $S = \max_i |P^*_i|$.

**The Relationship Argument (Conceptual Proof Sketch):**
The core idea to prove $S \ge G/2$ is to demonstrate that even if the greedy strategy $G$ keeps the partial sums oscillating *around* zero (i.e., $P'_i \approx 0$ for most $i$), there must be at least one significant excursion, $|P'_{k^*}| = G$. Furthermore, any arrangement $A^*$ that achieves $S$ must achieve a level of balance that cannot be fundamentally better than half the maximum deviation achieved by the most efficient zero-seeking process (George's).

1.  **Zero-Sum Property:** If the total sum $T = \sum a_i = 0$, then $P_m = P'_m = 0$. In this case, both $S$ and $G$ are determined by the maximum excursion before the last step.
2.  **The Fluctuation Constraint:** Consider the complete set of partial sums $\{P'_1, P'_2, \ldots, P'_m\}$ generated by George's sequence. Since the greedy strategy aggressively seeks to minimize $|P'_i|$, the total "span" of these sums (i.e., $G$) is minimized *given the local constraints*.
3.  **Connecting $G$ to $S$:** If $G$ is large, it means George's algorithm was forced to take a step that significantly increased the partial sum (an overshoot). An optimal arrangement $A^*$ must find a way to partition the set $A$ into prefixes $A^*_k$ such that the maximum of $|\sum A^*_k|$ is minimized.

**Hypothesis for Proof Completion:**
It is conjectured that the greedy algorithm $G$ represents the lowest possible price that can be achieved while satisfying the strong constraint of *local minimization* at every step. $S$ represents the absolute global minimum without such step-wise constraints. The factor of $1/2$ arises because a globally optimal sequence $A^*$ can always be constructed such that its maximum partial sum $S$ captures the necessary magnitude of the extreme overshoot $G$, but distributes it over two opposing phases of the sum. Specifically, if $G = |P'_{k^*}|$, then $P'_{k^*} \approx \sum_{i=1}^{k^*} a'_i$. The optimal sequence $A^*$ can likely balance this sum $P'_{k^*}$ by arranging the elements before and after $k^*$ to ensure the maximum deviation is no more than $G/2$.
