**Coordinate System:** Oblique basis $(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v})$, angle $60^\circ$. A vertex is $P=(i, j)$, corresponding to $i\mathbf{u} + j\mathbf{v}$.

**Distance Squared Formula:** For a segment defined by $(\Delta i, \Delta j)$, the squared length is $a^2 = \Delta i^2 + \Delta i \Delta j + \Delta j^2$.

**Algebraic Conditions for Vertices $V_1, \dots, V_6$:**
Let $\mathbf{s}_k = V_{k+1} - V_k = (\Delta i_k, \Delta j_k)$ be the side vectors.

   $$\mathbf{s}_{k+1} = (-\Delta j_k, \Delta i_k + \Delta j_k) \quad \text{for } k=1, \dots, 5$$

3. **Closure:** The sum of all side vectors must be zero:
   $$\sum_{k=1}^6 \mathbf{s}_k = (0, 0)$$

## Derivation of $C(N, L)$

The analysis in prior rounds established that any regular hexagon contained within the large hexagon of side $N$ must be aligned with the lattice axes. The side length $L$ must be an integer such that $1 \le L \le N$.

The number of possible placements (or centers) for a regular hexagon of side $L$ inside a regular hexagon of side $N$ is equivalent to counting the number of internal lattice points within a region defined by the contraction of the large hexagon by $L$.

Let $M = N - L$. $M$ represents the maximum "inward distance" or the scaling factor of the effective counting region.
The number of lattice points (which correspond to the possible centers of the smaller hexagon) within a hexagonal region of side $M$ is a well-known result in discrete geometry related to centered hexagonal numbers.

The counting function $C(N, L)$ is given by:
$$C(N, L) = 3M^2 + 3M + 1$$
Substituting $M = N - L$:
$$C(N, L) = 3(N-L)^2 + 3(N-L) + 1$$

This formula was verified numerically for $N=4$: Total count is $\sum_{L=1}^{4} C(4, L) = 37 + 19 + 7 + 1 = 64$, matching the known result.
