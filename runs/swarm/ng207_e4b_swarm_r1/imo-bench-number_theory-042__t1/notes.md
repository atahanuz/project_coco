**Analysis of the General Equation:**
The equation derived from $\tau(2k^3) = 2k$ is:
$$(3a+2) \cdot \prod_{i=1}^m (3e_i+1) = 2^{a+1} \cdot \prod_{i=1}^m p_i^{e_i}$$
where $k = 2^a p_1^{e_1} \cdots p_m^{e_m}$.

**Strong Necessary Conditions:**
We analyze the prime factorization structure of both sides, specifically separating the factors of 2 and the odd parts.

1.  **Condition on the Exponent of 2:**
    Let $V_2 = \sum_{i=1}^m v_2(3e_i+1)$, where $v_2(N)$ is the exponent of the highest power of 2 dividing $N$. Since $v_2(3a+2) + V_2$ must equal $a+1$, a necessary condition is:
    $$ v_2(3a+2) + \sum_{i=1}^m v_2(3e_i+1) = a+1 $$
    This heavily constrains the values of $a$ based on the exponents $e_i$.

2.  **Condition on Odd Factors (Structural Constraint on $e_i$):**
    Let $O_a = \text{odd}(3a+2)$. By equating the odd parts of both sides, we must have:
    $$ O_a \cdot \prod_{i=1}^m \text{odd}(3e_i+1) = \prod_{i=1}^m p_i^{e_i} $$
    This is a very strong structural necessary condition. It implies that the odd part of $k$ ($\prod p_i^{e_i}$) must be exactly equal to the product of $O_a$ and the odd parts generated by the divisor function terms $\text{odd}(3e_i+1)$.

    A consequence of this is that for every odd prime factor $p_i$ of $k$, $p_i$ must divide $O_a$ or $p_i$ must divide at least one factor $\text{odd}(3e_j+1)$.
