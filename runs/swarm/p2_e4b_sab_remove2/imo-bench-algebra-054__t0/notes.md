**Investigation of Minimal Complexity in Non-Periodic Sequences**

1.  **Complexity Function $p(k)$:** For an infinite sequence $a_n$ over a finite alphabet $\Sigma$, the complexity function $p(k)$ is defined as the number of distinct subwords (factors) of length $k$ appearing in the sequence.
2.  **Aperiodicity and Growth:** If a sequence is eventually periodic, its complexity $p(k)$ is bounded by the length of the period and eventually constant. Since the sequence $a_n$ is *not* eventually periodic, $p(k)$ must grow without bound as $k \to \infty$.
3.  **Minimal Complexity (Binary Case):** For a sequence over a binary alphabet ($\Sigma = \{0, 1\}$), the minimal complexity for an aperiodic sequence is achieved by Sturmian sequences. A sequence $a_n$ is Sturmian if and only if $p(k) = k+1$ for all $k \ge 1$. This rate of growth ($p(k) \sim k$) is the slowest possible for a non-periodic sequence over two symbols.
4.  **Minimal Complexity (General Case):** If the alphabet size $|\Sigma| = m > 2$, the minimal growth rate is generally higher than $k+1$ for large $k$, or the sequence must be highly structured (e.g., generated by substitution rules that maintain low complexity). However, since we seek the *absolute minimum* number of factors, we assume the alphabet is chosen to be the smallest possible, which is $m=2$ (binary), as this yields the lowest growth rate $p(k)=k+1$.

**Conclusion for the Task:** The minimum number of distinct factors of length $k$ for a non-periodic sequence over a finite alphabet occurs when the sequence is Sturmian (assuming a binary alphabet).
