### Analysis of Permutation Distance on $C_N$ (Ignoring Rotation)

**1. Linear Case (Path $P_N$):**
When the vertices are arranged linearly, the minimum number of adjacent swaps required to transform one permutation $\pi_A$ to another $\pi_B$ is equal to the number of inversions in the permutation $\sigma$ defined by $\pi_B = \sigma \circ \pi_A$. This is the standard inversion distance.

**2. Cyclic Case ($C_N$) with Fixed Positions:**
When the vertices are arranged in a cycle $C_N$, and we fix the positions $1, \dots, N$, the distance $d(\pi_A, \pi_B)$ is defined by the minimum number of adjacent swaps necessary to transform the sequence $A=(a_1, \dots, a_N)$ into $B=(b_1, \dots, b_N)$.

Due to the circular nature, we can conceptually "cut" the cycle at any of the $N$ edges. Cutting the cycle at edge $k$ yields a linear sequence representation $A^{(k)}$ and $B^{(k)}$. The distance required for this specific linearization is the number of inversions in the permutation $\sigma_k$ that maps $A^{(k)}$ to $B^{(k)}$.

The minimum number of adjacent swaps required to transform $\pi_A$ to $\pi_B$ is:
$$d(\pi_A, \pi_B) = \min_{k=1}^N \{\text{Inversion Distance}(A^{(k)}, B^{(k)})\}.$$

**Summary:** The distance is related to the inversion distance, but specifically, it is the minimum inversion distance achievable across all $N$ possible ways of unrolling the cycle into a line.
