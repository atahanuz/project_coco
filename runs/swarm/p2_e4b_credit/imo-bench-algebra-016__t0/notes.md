Initial analysis of George's Greedy Strategy (Price G):
1. Initialization (i=1): $a_1$ is chosen to minimize $|a_1| = \min_{a \in A} |a|$.
2. Step i (i>1): Let $P_{i-1} = a_1 + \cdots + a_{i-1}$. George chooses $a_i$ from the remaining set $A_{rem}$ such that $|P_{i-1} + a_i|$ is minimized.
This means $a_i$ is the element in $A_{rem}$ closest to $-P_{i-1}$.

The price $G$ is defined as $G = \max_{1\le k\le m}|P_k|$.

To characterize the sequence, we need to understand how the partial sums $P_k$ evolve. Since $a_i$ is chosen to minimize $|P_i|$, the partial sums will be kept as close to zero as possible at each step. This suggests $G$ might be related to the smallest possible maximum absolute partial sum achievable by any arrangement, i.e., $G$ might be close to $S$.

Analysis of Minimum Price S:
1. Optimal Sequence Construction: To minimize $S = \max_{k}|P_k|$, where $P_k$ is the $k$-th partial sum, the optimal arrangement generally involves keeping the running sum close to zero. This is achieved by interleaving elements of opposing signs, particularly pairing the largest remaining positive number with the largest remaining negative number. If all numbers share the same sign, the minimum price is simply the absolute value of the total sum, $S = |\sum a_i|$.

2. General Lower Bound for S: Since the final partial sum $P_m$ must equal the total sum $T = \sum a_i$, and $S$ is the maximum absolute value of any partial sum, a fundamental lower bound must be:
$$S \ge |\sum_{i=1}^m a_i|.$$
This bound is independent of the arrangement chosen. Further, if the numbers are highly unbalanced (e.g., large positive sum $T^+$ and small negative sum $T^-$), $S$ will be dominated by $|T^+|$, and the bound might approach $S \approx \max(T^+, |T^-|)$ in specific cases, but $S \ge |T|$ is the most robust general bound based purely on the set sum.
