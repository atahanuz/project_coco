The initial numbers are x_0 = m/(m+1) and y_0 = (m+1)/m. These are reciprocals (x_0 * y_0 = 1).

When two numbers are reciprocals, applying the Arithmetic Mean (AM) repeatedly, $A(a, b) = (a+b)/2$, generates the sequence $a_{k+1} = (a_k + 1/a_k)/2$. This is the Babylonian method for approximating sqrt(1) = 1.
Similarly, applying the Harmonic Mean (HM) leads to results related to the AM sequence.

For any starting pair of distinct positive reciprocals (like m/(m+1) and (m+1)/m where m>=1), the sequence of numbers generated by repeated AM/HM operations converges to 1.

However, since the initial numbers are not equal to 1, and the arithmetic/harmonic means of rational numbers are rational numbers, achieving the exact value 1 requires an infinite number of steps unless m=n=1 (which is trivial and violates the structure of a starting pair having two distinct values).

Conclusion for the task: For pairs (m, n) such that |m-n|=1, 1 cannot be written on the board in finitely many steps. This suggests that solutions to the overall problem likely require cases where |m-n| > 1.
