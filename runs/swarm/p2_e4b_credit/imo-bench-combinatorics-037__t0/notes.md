### [W2, round 1]
Bachata Set Definition: $S$ is Bachata if $0 \in S$, and for $n \in S$, we have $n+11 \in S$ and $n+20 \in S$.

Task Focus: Determine conditions on representatives $r_i \in \{0, \dots, 10\}$ imposed by $n \in S \implies n+20 \in S$.

Assumption: Due to the structure ($+11$ and $+20$), we assume $S$ is defined by a set of residues $R \subseteq \{0, 1, \ldots, 10\}$, such that $S = \{ n \mid n \equiv r \pmod{11} \text{ for some } r \in R \}$.

1. $0 \in S$ implies $0 \pmod{11} \in R$, so $0 \in R$.
2. The condition $n \in S \implies n+20 \in S$ translates to a condition on the residues. Since $20 \equiv 9 \pmod{11}$, this means:
   If $r \in R$, then $r+9 \pmod{11} \in R$.

3. Let $f(r) = r+9 \pmod{11}$. We analyze the cycles of $f$:
   $0 \xrightarrow{f} 9 \xrightarrow{f} 7 \xrightarrow{f} 5 \xrightarrow{f} 3 \xrightarrow{f} 1 \xrightarrow{f} 10 \xrightarrow{f} 8 \xrightarrow{f} 6 \xrightarrow{f} 4 \xrightarrow{f} 2 \xrightarrow{f} 0$.
   This is a single cycle of length 11 covering all elements $\{0, 1, \ldots, 10\}$.

4. For $R$ to be closed under $f$, and since we require $0 \in R$, $R$ must contain the entire cycle generated by 0.
   Therefore, $R$ must be $\{0, 1, \ldots, 10\}$.

Conclusion: The necessary and sufficient condition derived from the constraint $n \in S \implies n+20 \in S$, combined with $0 \in S$, is that the set of representatives must be all of $\{0, 1, \ldots, 10\}$.
