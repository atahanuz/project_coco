### [W3, round 1]
Analysis of George's Greedy Strategy:

1. Formalization: At step $i$, given the set of remaining numbers $R$ and the current prefix sum $P_{i-1}$, George chooses $a_i$ such that:
$$a_i = \arg \min_{x \in R} |P_{i-1} + x|$$

2. Proof of Minimality: This choice is definitionally correct. By selecting $a_i$ to be the element in $R$ that minimizes the expression $|P_{i-1} + x|$, the resulting prefix sum $|P_i| = |P_{i-1} + a_i|$ achieves the smallest possible absolute value attainable using any available number $x \in R$.

3. Sequence Structure: This greedy strategy attempts to keep the running prefix sum $P_i$ as close to zero as possible at every stage. The resulting sequence structure is highly balanced. If the input numbers are centered around zero (e.g., $\{-3, -1, 1, 3\}$), the sequence will tend to alternate signs or group small magnitude numbers near the sum zero to maintain a low 'price'. If the numbers are heavily skewed (e.g., all positive), the sequence will still attempt to keep the sum growing as slowly as possible by repeatedly picking the smallest available number.

### [W2, round 1]
### Investigation of Optimal Price (S) Minimization Strategy

The goal is to minimize $S = \min_{\text{arrangements}} \left( \max_{1\le k\le m} |\sum_{i=1}^k a_i| \right)$. This requires keeping all prefix sums close to zero.

1. **Principle:** The optimal arrangement must balance positive and negative contributions to the running sum. Alternating between large positive and large negative numbers is the primary strategy to keep the sum bounded and oscillating around zero.

2. **Sorting Hypothesis:** Let the input set be $A$.
    a. Sort $A$ by magnitude: $|x|_{(1)} \le |x|_{(2)} \le \ldots \le |x|_{(m)}$.
    b. Sort $A$ by value: $x_{(1)} \le x_{(2)} \le \ldots \le x_{(m)}$.

3. **Conjecture (Alternating Extremes):** The optimal strategy for minimizing $S$ is generally achieved by arranging the numbers such that large positive and large negative values alternate. Specifically, if we partition the numbers into positives ($P$) and non-positives ($N$), we should arrange them by alternating the largest magnitude remaining element from $P$ and $N$.

4. **Specific Pattern:** A highly effective pattern often involves sorting the values $x_{(1)} \le x_{(2)} \le \ldots \le x_{(m)}$ and constructing the sequence $a$ by interleaving the lower half and the upper half (e.g., $x_{(1)}, x_{(m)}, x_{(2)}, x_{(m-1)}, x_{(3)}, x_{(m-2)}, \ldots$). This strategy ensures that the largest swings are countered by values of opposite sign/large magnitude.

This suggests the optimal arrangement depends heavily on balancing the extremes of the set, rather than strictly grouping numbers of the same sign or magnitude.

### [W4, round 1]
Conjecture for k: k=1.

Preliminary Reasoning:
1. S is the minimum possible price over all permutations of the input numbers.
2. G is the price of the sequence generated by George's specific greedy algorithm.
3. By definition, the minimum price S must be less than or equal to any specific price, including G. Thus, $S \le G$.
4. We seek the maximum k such that $S \ge kG$ holds for all inputs. This requires $k \le S/G$.
5. Since $S/G \le 1$, the absolute maximum value k can take is 1. This conjecture is based on the strong possibility that $S=G$ for many, or perhaps all, inputs, allowing the supremum $k=1$ to be reached.
