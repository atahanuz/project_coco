### [W2, round 2] --- Analysis of Divisibility Condition for n=4 ---

The recurrence relation requires $x_4 = \frac{x_3^2 - x_2}{x_2 + 1}$ to be a positive integer. This implies the necessary divisibility condition: $(x_2 + 1) \mid (x_3^2 - x_2)$.

**1. Expressing the Condition:**
Using modular arithmetic, we analyze $x_3^2 - x_2 \pmod{x_2 + 1}$. Since $x_2 \equiv -1 \pmod{x_2 + 1}$, we substitute:
$x_3^2 - x_2 \equiv x_3^2 - (-1) \equiv x_3^2 + 1 \pmod{x_2 + 1}$.
Therefore, the divisibility condition $(x_2 + 1) \mid (x_3^2 - x_2)$ is equivalent to requiring that $(x_2 + 1)$ must be a divisor of $(x_3^2 + 1)$.

**2. Structural Constraints (Violations):**
A violation occurs if and only if $x_2 + 1$ is NOT a divisor of $x_3^2 + 1$.
For any chosen positive integer $x_3$, any choice of $x_2$ such that $x_2 + 1$ is not among the divisors of $x_3^2 + 1$ will violate the condition.
To ensure $x_4$ is an integer, $x_2$ must satisfy $x_2 = d - 1$, where $d$ is a divisor of $x_3^2 + 1$ and $d \ge 2$ (since $x_2 \ge 1$).
