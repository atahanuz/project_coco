The core conflict is between the spreading nature of the flood and the constrained growth of Sun Wukong's barrier. Sun Wukong wins if he can enclose all flooded cells ($F$) within a closed loop ($L$) before $F$ spreads indefinitely outside $L$.

The greatest challenge arises from initial flooded configurations ($F_{initial}$) that possess:

1.  **High Perimeter-to-Area Ratio:** Configurations that are long, thin, or highly fragmented (like a long chain or a set of scattered clusters) require a significantly longer perimeter barrier $L$ to contain them, even if the total area $|F_{initial}|$ is small. This immediately demands a higher $\gamma$.
2.  **Dispersion and Multi-Directional Threat:** If $F_{initial}$ is dispersed, Sun Wukong must build several segments of the barrier simultaneously to prevent the flood from escaping in multiple directions. This prevents the efficient closure of a single, compact loop. The barrier resources ($\gamma n$) must be distributed across many fronts, potentially leading to a slower overall enclosure rate compared to a compact initial seed.

In summary, the worst-case configurations are those that are geometrically complex, forcing the barrier to grow rapidly and extensively along high-perimeter features, rather than simply closing a small, compact area.

### Geometric Requirements: Perimeter vs. Area Scaling

**1. Isoperimetric Baseline:**
For any simply connected region $F$ (the flooded area) on the grid, the minimum perimeter $P(F)$ required to contain it scales according to the isoperimetric inequality. Specifically, $P(F) \gtrsim k \cdot \sqrt{|F|}$, where $|F|$ is the area and $k$ is a constant (e.g., $k \approx 4$ for a compact square region). This defines the ideal, minimum required barrier length for a given enclosed area.

**2. Dynamic Constraint:**
Sun Wukong's available barrier length after $n$ turns is $L_n \leq \gamma n$. To guarantee victory, Wukong must ensure $L_n \geq P(F(n))$ at all times $n$.

**3. Necessary Condition for $\gamma$ (Compact Growth):**
If the flood grows compactly (e.g., maintaining a roughly circular shape, so $|F(n)| \propto n^2$), then $P(F(n)) \propto \sqrt{n^2} = n$. In this case, Wukong requires $\gamma \gtrsim k$ to keep pace with the expanding boundary.

**4. Critical Worst-Case Scenario (Dispersion):**
The Sea God's ability to choose the initial configuration and exploit the adjacency rule introduces a high-perimeter-to-area ratio threat. If the flood expands into many small, scattered clusters (high dispersion) rather than one large, compact cluster, the total required perimeter $P(F(n))$ increases disproportionately faster than $\sqrt{|F(n)|}$.

If the Sea God can force the flood to occupy $m$ clusters, where $m$ grows faster than $O(1)$, the total required barrier length $L$ scales roughly with $m \cdot \sqrt{|F|/m}$. If $m$ grows too fast relative to $n$, the required barrier length $L$ can exceed $O(n)$, making victory impossible regardless of how large $\gamma$ is chosen.

**Conclusion for Task:**
The geometric requirement dictates that $\gamma$ must be large enough to overcome the worst-case boundary complexity generated by the Sea God's initial placement and subsequent expansion strategy (dispersion). The relationship is that $\gamma$ must be greater than the maximum possible perimeter-to-time ratio $\sup_n \frac{P(F(n))}{n}$ induced by the SG.

$$P_{flood}(n) \sim O(\sqrt{A_{flood}(n)}) \sim O(n)$$

$$L_{barrier}(n) \gtrsim k \cdot n$$

**3. Determining $\gamma$:**
The constraint given is $L_{barrier}(n) \leq \gamma n$.
For Wukong to guarantee victory, the constraint must allow for the necessary growth:
$$\gamma n \gtrsim k \cdot n$$
This suggests that the minimum required scaling factor $C$ must satisfy $C \geq k$.

**Conclusion:** The critical scaling factor $\gamma$ must be at least proportional to the constant $k$ required to trace the perimeter of a worst-case expanding flood region. ### [W2, round 2] Formal Determination of the Constant C

**Objective:** Rigorously determine $\sup_n \frac{P(F(n))}{n}$ achievable by the Sea God (SG) to set the upper bound $C$.

**Analysis:**
1.  **Geometric Upper Bound on Perimeter:** For any finite set of connected unit squares $F$ on the grid, the perimeter $P(F)$ is bounded by $P(F) \le 2|F| + 2$, where $|F|$ is the area (number of cells). This maximum perimeter-to-area ratio ($P/|F| \to 2$) is achieved when $F$ forms a single, long, thin strip (a path).
2.  **Dynamic Upper Bound on Area:** The flood spreads based on adjacency. Starting from a finite initial set $F_{init}$, the area $F(n)$ grows over $n$ turns.
    *   The fastest possible area growth rate is quadratic ($|F(n)| \sim O(n^2)$) if the flood expands into a compact shape (disk).
    *   The slowest area growth rate that still allows the flood to spread indefinitely (required for the $\sup_n$ limit) is linear ($|F(n)| \sim O(n)$), achieved when the flood occupies a single, winding path of unit width.
3.  **Worst-Case Ratio Maximization:** The Sea God maximizes the ratio $\frac{P(F(n))}{n}$ by forcing the flood to occupy a path that simultaneously maximizes $P(F(n))/|F(n)|$ and minimizes $|F(n)|/n$.
    *   The absolute maximum of $P/|F|$ is 2.
    *   If the SG can force the flood to grow as a path of length $L \sim n$ (i.e., $|F(n)| \sim n$), then:
        $$P(F(n)) \le 2|F(n)| + 2 \approx 2n$$
        $$\frac{P(F(n))}{n} \le \frac{2n + 2}{n} \to 2 \quad \text{as } n \to \infty$$

**Conclusion:**
The maximum possible ratio $\sup_n \frac{P(F(n))}{n}$ is 2. Since Sun Wukong needs $\gamma > \sup_n \frac{P(F(n))}{n}$ to guarantee victory against the worst-case expansion, the largest constant $C$ that guarantees victory for all $\gamma > C$ is $C=2$. The existence of "extra walls" does not change this asymptotic bound, as the SG's worst-case strategy is defined by the expansion mechanics based on $F_{init}$.

### [W3, round 2] Adversarial Configuration Analysis: Maximizing P(F(n))/n

**Objective:** Construct a flood configuration that maximizes the ratio of required barrier length $P(F_n)$ to the number of Sun Wukong's turns $n$, thereby establishing a lower bound for $C$.

1.  **Initial State ($n=0$):** $F_0$ is a single cell.
2.  **Time Progression:** In the absence of walls (or if walls are insufficient), the flood expands isotropically (in all directions) in each of the Sea God's turns.

4.  **Perimeter Requirement:** For $F_n$ to be contained, Sun Wukong (SW) must build walls along the perimeter, $P(F_n)$. For an $N \times N$ square, the perimeter is $P(F_n) = 4N$.
5.  **Scaling:** Since $N \sim O(n)$ under this highly compact, maximal-perimeter-growth adversarial strategy, the required barrier length scales as:
    $$P(F_n) \sim O(n)$$
    Specifically, if we assume the flood successfully grows to a side length $N \approx n$ (assuming unit spread per turn), then $P(F_n) \approx 4n$.

**Conclusion and Lower Bound for C:**
For Sun Wukong to guarantee victory, his capacity must meet the required boundary length:
$$L_n \geq P(F_n)$$
$$\gamma n \geq 4n \quad (\text{for large } n)$$
