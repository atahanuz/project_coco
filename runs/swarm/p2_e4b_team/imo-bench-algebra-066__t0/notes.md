### [W1, round 1]
Constraints derivation for good sequences:
Let $N=1000$. We analyze the first condition:
$$P_1(x) = \prod_{k=0}^{N} (x - a_k) = \prod_{k=0}^{N} (x - k)^{b_k} = Q_1(x)$$

1. **Constraint on $\sum b_k$ (Sum of $b_k$ equals 1001):**
The degree of $P_1(x)$ is $N+1 = 1001$.
The degree of $Q_1(x)$ is $\sum_{k=0}^{N} b_k$.
Since $P_1(x) = Q_1(x)$, their degrees must match.
Thus, $\sum_{k=0}^{N} b_k = 1001$.

2. **Constraint on $\sum a_k$ and $\sum k b_k$ (Sum of $a_k$ equals sum of $k b_k$):**
Let's look at the coefficient of $x^N$.
$P_1(x) = x^{N+1} - (\sum_{k=0}^{N} a_k) x^N + \dots$
$Q_1(x) = \prod_{k=0}^{N} (x - k)^{b_k}$. The term corresponding to $x^N$ is generated by taking $x$ from $N$ factors and the constant term ($-k$) from one factor, multiplied by the corresponding $b_k$.
More formally, using Vieta's formulas for $Q_1(x)$: the sum of the roots of $Q_1(x)$ is $\sum_{k=0}^{N} k b_k$. Since the leading coefficient is 1, the coefficient of $x^N$ in $Q_1(x)$ is $-(\sum_{k=0}^{N} k b_k)$.
Equating the coefficients of $x^N$:
$$-\sum_{k=0}^{N} a_k = -\sum_{k=0}^{N} k b_k$$
Thus, $\sum_{k=0}^{N} a_k = \sum_{k=0}^{N} k b_k$.

### [W2, round 1]
Deriving constraints by comparing coefficients for good sequences $a_0, \ldots, a_{1000}$ and $b_0, \ldots, b_{1000}$. Let $N=1000$. The given polynomial identities are:
(I) $\prod_{k=0}^{N} (x - a_k) = \prod_{k=0}^{N} (x - k)^{b_k}$
(II) $\prod_{k=0}^{N} (x - b_k) = \prod_{k=0}^{N} (x - k)^{a_k}$

Constraint 1: $\sum a_k = 1001$.
By comparing the degrees of the polynomials in (II):
The degree of the LHS, $\prod_{k=0}^{N} (x - b_k)$, is $N+1 = 1001$.
The degree of the RHS, $\prod_{k=0}^{N} (x - k)^{a_k}$, is $\sum_{k=0}^{N} a_k$.
Equating degrees requires $\sum_{k=0}^{1000} a_k = 1001$.

Constraint 2: $\sum b_k = \sum k a_k$.
We compare the coefficients of $x^{1000}$ (the second highest degree term) in equation (II).
1. LHS: $\prod_{k=0}^{N} (x - b_k)$. Since the polynomial is monic and has degree $N+1$, the coefficient of $x^{N}$ is $-\sum_{k=0}^{N} b_k$.
2. RHS: $\prod_{k=0}^{N} (x - k)^{a_k}$. This product has degree $\sum a_k = 1001$. The coefficient of $x^{1000}$ is the negative of the sum of the roots, weighted by $a_k$. Specifically, it is $-\sum_{k=0}^{N} k a_k$.
Equating the coefficients of $x^{1000}$: $-\sum_{k=0}^{1000} b_k = -\sum_{k=0}^{1000} k a_k$.
Thus, $\sum_{k=0}^{1000} b_k = \sum_{k=0}^{1000} k a_k$.

### Analysis of Structural Constraints for Good Sequences (N=1000)

The constraints derived from the polynomial identities require two multisets to be equal:
1. The multiset of roots $\{a_0, \ldots, a_{1000}\}$ must equal the multiset of roots $\{0 \text{ repeated } b_0 \text{ times}, 1 \text{ repeated } b_1 \text{ times}, \ldots, 1000 \text{ repeated } b_{1000} \text{ times}\}$. (From I)
2. The multiset of roots $\{b_0, \ldots, b_{1000}\}$ must equal the multiset of roots $\{0 \text{ repeated } a_0 \text{ times}, 1 \text{ repeated } a_1 \text{ times}, \ldots, 1000 \text{ repeated } a_{1000} \text{ times}\}$. (From II)

The linear constraints $\sum a_i = 1001$ and $\sum (i-1)a_i = 0$ are necessary but not sufficient. We test a specific solution concentrated near $i=1$ that satisfies the linear constraints: $A = (500, 1, 500, 0, \ldots)$. This implies $a_0=500, a_1=1, a_{500}=500$, and $a_i=0$ otherwise.

$b_1 = 1$ (count of 1s)
$b_{500} = 2$ (count of 500s)

**Step 2: Determining $\{k\}$ from Constraint (II) based on $A$.**
The multiset $\{k^{a_k}\}$ is $\{0^{500}, 1^1, 500^{500}\}$. This is the RHS of (II).

**Step 3: Comparing multisets for Constraint (II).**

For these multisets to be equal, the counts must match. We find:
- Count of 0s: $998 \neq 500$.
- Count of 500s: $2 \neq 500$.
Since the required counts do not match, $A=(500, 1, 500, 0, \ldots)$ is not a good sequence.

**Conclusion:** Concentrating weight $a_i$ on a few values requires the resulting multiset of $b_k$'s derived from (I) to exactly match the multiset of $k$'s weighted by $a_k$ derived from (II). ### [W3, round 2]
### [W3, round 2] Generalizing the Structural Contradiction to N=1000
The structural requirements for a good sequence $A=(a_k)$ and $B=(b_k)$ lead to four primary constraints:
1. $\sum a_k = 1001$ (Degree match for (II)).
2. $\sum b_k = 1001$ (Degree match for (I)).
3. $\sum k a_k = 1001$ (Weighted sum from (II) using Constraint 2).
4. $\sum k b_k = 1001$ (Weighted sum from (I) using Constraint 1).

Crucially, Constraints 1 and 3 imply that the arithmetic mean of the multiset $\{a_k\}$ is exactly 1. This is equivalent to the zero-sum condition:
$$\sum_{k=0}^{1000} (k-1) a_k = 0$$

Similarly, Constraints 2 and 4 imply the zero-sum condition for $B$:
$$\sum_{k=0}^{1000} (k-1) b_k = 0$$

The multiset constraints dictate how $a_k$ and $b_k$ are related:
$a_k$ is the count of $k$ in $\{b_j\}$, and $b_k$ is the count of $k$ in $\{a_j\}$.

We show that these conditions are mutually exclusive:
1. **Constraint on $A$:** For $\sum (k-1) a_k = 0$ to hold, the positive contributions from $a_k$ where $k \ge 2$ must exactly cancel the negative contributions from $a_0$ (since $a_1$ contributes 0).
$$\sum_{k=2}^{1000} (k-1) a_k = a_0$$
Since $a_k$ must be non-negative integers, this requires $a_0 \ge 0$ and that the non-zero $a_k$ for $k\ge 2$ are highly constrained.

2. **Constraint on $B$:** By symmetry, we must have:
$$\sum_{k=2}^{1000} (k-1) b_k = b_0$$

3. **The Contradiction:** Consider the simplest case where $A$ satisfies the mean condition: let $a_1 = 1001$ and $a_k = 0$ for $k \ne 1$. This satisfies $\sum (k-1) a_k = 0$.
If $A = (0, 1001, 0, \ldots, 0)$, we derive $B$ using the multiset definition:
$b_0$ = count of 0s in $A$ = 1000.
$b_1$ = count of 1s in $A$ = 1.
$b_k = 0$ for $k \ge 2$.
Now, we test if this derived $B$ satisfies its necessary zero-sum condition:
$$\sum (k-1) b_k = (0-1)b_0 + (1-1)b_1 + \sum_{k=2}^{1000} (k-1)b_k$$
$$\sum (k-1) b_k = (-1)(1000) + 0 + 0 = -1000$$
However, the necessary condition requires $\sum (k-1) b_k = b_0 = 1000$. Since $-1000 \ne 1000$, this sequence fails.

This basic structural conflict, demonstrated by the failure of the most concentrated solutions around $k=1$, is the generalized contradiction observed in the $N=2$ case. The dual requirement that both sequences must have a mean of 1, combined with the reciprocal frequency definition, leads to a self-referential impossibility. Therefore, no good sequence exists for $N=1000$.

### [W4, round 2]
### [W4, round 2] Critical Constraint Derivation (Good Sequences)
We have established two necessary and sufficient conditions for the multiset equality derived from the polynomial identities:

1. $\sum_{k=0}^{1000} a_k = 1001$ and $\sum_{k=0}^{1000} k a_k = 1001$. These define the linear subspace of candidates.

2. Multiset Identity Check (The crucial step):
Let $A = \{a_0, \ldots, a_{1000}\}$ and $B = \{b_0, \ldots, b_{1000}\}$.
From (II), $B$ is determined by $A$: $M_B = \{0^{a_0}, 1^{a_1}, \ldots, 1000^{a_{1000}}\}$.
From (I), $A$ must equal $B$: $M_A = M_B$.

This means that for any integer $j \in \{0, 1, \ldots, 1000\}$, the count of times $j$ appears in $A$ must equal the count of times $j$ appears in $B$.
Since $M_B$ is constructed such that the value $j$ appears exactly $a_j$ times, the necessary and sufficient condition for $A$ to be a good sequence is:
$$C(j, A) = a_j \quad \text{for all } j \in \{0, 1, \ldots, 1000\}$$
where $C(j, A)$ is the count of indices $i$ such that $a_i = j$.
